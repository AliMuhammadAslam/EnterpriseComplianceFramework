# Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP) — Fintech Regulations

## Overview
The Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP) regulates the corporate sector, capital markets, insurance, NBFCs, and private pensions. For fintech, SECP's role covers digital lending by NBFCs, regulatory sandbox, and insurtech. Key legislation includes the Companies Act 2017, NBFC Rules 2003 (amended), and the SECP Regulatory Sandbox Guidelines 2019.

## Scope
Applies to Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs), investment advisors, insurance companies, and fintech entities operating in capital markets, lending (outside SBP jurisdiction), and insurance under SECP licensing.

## NBFC and Digital Lending Framework

### Licensing Categories
- The NBFC Regulations 2008 regulate NBFCs carrying out leasing, investment finance services, housing finance services, discounting services, micro financing, asset management services and investment advisory services, including Non-Banking Microfinance Companies (NBMFCs)

### Digital Lending Requirements
- Minimum equity requirement as per NBFC category; the NBFC Regulations 2008 define "Digital Lending" and set separate minimum equity amounts for NBFCs undertaking it
- A Key Fact Statement must be provided before disbursement, and any fee not included in it cannot be charged (SECP Circular No. 15 of 2022, as described in SECP's announcement)
- NBFCs must verify the borrower's identity through biometric verification (NBFC Regulations 2008)

### Consumer Protection
- Peer-to-peer (P2P) lending is regulated under the NBFC Regulations 2008: P2P service providers must disclose all loan terms and conditions, including likely return, fees and taxes, and must have Board-approved mechanisms for recovering loans while avoiding harassment and coercion, and for grievance redressal within set timelines (regulations 35I and 35L)

## Regulatory Sandbox Framework (2019)

### Purpose
Allows fintech companies to test innovative financial products and services in a controlled environment under SECP supervision, with relaxed regulatory requirements for a limited period.

### Eligibility
- Applications may be made by companies registered with SECP (including licensed entities, fintech companies and technology providers), by presently unregistered startups intending to register if testing succeeds, and by internationally recognised companies or other persons the Commission deems fit (SECP Regulatory Sandbox Guidelines, 2019)
- Innovative product or service not adequately addressed by existing regulation
- Demonstrable benefit to consumers or the financial system
- Adequate risk mitigation plan

### Sandbox Conditions
- Testing period: up to six months from the letter of approval; the Commission may extend it on the participant's request where it believes on reasonable grounds that an extension is suitable
- The letter of approval sets out the agreed consumer safeguards and the content and frequency of reports to be submitted to SECP
- Applicants must submit an exit plan; at the exit stage SECP reviews the completion report and decides whether to facilitate scaling up in a compliant manner (or an orderly wind-up) and whether to formulate or amend the regulatory framework

## Corporate Governance Requirements

### Board Composition
- Independent directors: at least two or one third of the members of the Board, whichever is higher (Listed Companies (Code of Corporate Governance) Regulations, 2019, regulation 6)
- Audit Committee of at least three members comprising non-executive directors and at least one independent director, chaired by an independent director who is not the chairman of the Board (regulation 27)
- The Board may constitute a Risk Management Committee (regulation 30)
- Related party transactions are placed before the audit committee and, on its recommendation, before the Board for review and approval; where a majority of directors are interested, the matter goes to the general meeting (regulation 15)

### Reporting and Disclosure
- The chief executive officer and chief financial officer must endorse the quarterly, half-yearly and annual financial statements before they are placed before the Board (Code of Corporate Governance, regulation 25)

## Anti-Money Laundering Obligations
- SECP-regulated entities must comply with AML/CFT regulations
- Customer Due Diligence matching FATF Recommendations
- Reporting of suspicious and currency transactions to the Financial Monitoring Unit (FMU) (SECP AML/CFT/CPF Regulations, 2020)
- Beneficial ownership disclosure for all corporate entities
- Ongoing monitoring of business relationships

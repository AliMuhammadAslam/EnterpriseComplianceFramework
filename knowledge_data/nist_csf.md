# NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

## Overview
The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) is a voluntary framework developed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in the United States. Originally published in 2014 and updated to version 2.0 in 2024, it provides a structured approach to managing cybersecurity risk. While originally designed for critical infrastructure, it is widely adopted across industries globally.

## Scope
Applicable to organizations of any size, sector, or maturity level. The framework is technology-neutral and designed to complement existing cybersecurity programs. It is referenced by many regulatory bodies and industry standards worldwide.

## Core Functions (NIST CSF 2.0)

### Govern (GV) — New in 2.0
- Establish and monitor cybersecurity risk management strategy, expectations, and policy
- Organizational context and risk management strategy
- Cybersecurity supply chain risk management
- Roles, responsibilities, and authorities
- Policies, processes, and procedures
- Oversight and governance

### Identify (ID)
- Asset management: Identify and manage physical and software assets
- Business environment and objectives
- Risk assessment: Identify cybersecurity risks to operations, assets, and individuals
- Improvement: Identify improvements to organizational cybersecurity practices

### Protect (PR)
- Identity management and access control
- Awareness and training programs
- Data security: Protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data
- Platform security: Manage hardware, software, and services
- Technology infrastructure resilience

### Detect (DE)
- Continuous monitoring: Monitor for cybersecurity events
- Adverse event analysis: Analyze anomalies and potential cybersecurity events
- Security event correlation and alerting

### Respond (RS)
- Incident management: Execute response plan during or after an incident
- Incident analysis and forensics
- Incident response reporting and communication
- Incident mitigation and containment

### Recover (RC)
- Incident recovery plan execution
- Recovery communication
- Restoration of systems and assets

## Implementation Tiers
- **Tier 1 (Partial)**: Informal, reactive risk management
- **Tier 2 (Risk Informed)**: Risk management practices approved by management but not organization-wide
- **Tier 3 (Repeatable)**: Formal, organization-wide risk management policies
- **Tier 4 (Adaptive)**: Organization adapts cybersecurity practices based on lessons learned and predictive indicators

## Key Components
- **Framework Core**: Set of cybersecurity activities organized into Functions, Categories, and Subcategories
- **Framework Profiles**: Alignment of the Functions, Categories, and Subcategories with business requirements, risk tolerance, and resources
- **Implementation Tiers**: Characterize an organization's cybersecurity risk management practices

## NIST Special Publications (Related)
- **NIST SP 800-53**: Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems
- **NIST SP 800-171**: Protecting Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) in Nonfederal Systems
- **NIST SP 800-37**: Risk Management Framework (RMF)

## Compliance and Enforcement
- NIST CSF is voluntary for most private-sector organizations
- Mandatory for U.S. federal agencies (via Executive Orders)
- Often required by industry-specific regulations
- Used as a benchmark in regulatory assessments
- Non-compliance may result in increased risk exposure and liability

# Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA) 2016 — Pakistan

## Overview
The Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016 (PECA) is Pakistan's primary cybercrime legislation. It criminalizes unauthorized access to information systems, data breaches, electronic fraud, identity theft, and cyber terrorism. For fintech companies, PECA establishes the legal framework for prosecuting cybercrimes targeting financial systems and sets obligations for data protection and system security.

## Scope
Applies to any person who commits an offence under this Act within or outside Pakistan, where the offence involves a system, data, or communication in Pakistan, or causes harm to a person or entity in Pakistan.

## Key Offences Relevant to Fintech

### Unauthorized Access (Section 3)
- Unauthorized access to any information system or data
- Penalty: imprisonment up to 3 months, or fine up to PKR 50,000, or both
- Section 3 has no aggravated form. Unauthorized access to a critical infrastructure information system or data is a separate offence under Section 6: imprisonment up to three years, or fine up to PKR 1 million, or both

### Unauthorized Copying or Transmission of Data (Section 4)
- Copying or transmitting data from any information system without authorization
- Penalty: imprisonment up to 6 months, or fine up to PKR 100,000, or both
- Particularly relevant to fintech data breaches and insider threats

### System Interference (Section 5)
- Intentionally interfering with or damaging an information system
- Includes DDoS attacks, malware deployment, or sabotage of financial systems
- Penalty: imprisonment up to 2 years, or fine up to PKR 500,000, or both

### Electronic Fraud (Section 14)
- Using any information system to interfere with any data or system with dishonest intent
- Covers phishing, card skimming, unauthorized transaction manipulation
- Penalty: imprisonment up to 2 years, or fine up to PKR 10 million, or both

### Electronic Forgery (Section 13)
- Creating or altering electronic documents, records, or data with intent to defraud
- Covers forged digital signatures, altered financial records
- Penalty: imprisonment up to 3 years, or fine up to PKR 250,000, or both

### Identity Theft (Section 16)
- Unauthorized use of another person's identity information
- Relevant to KYC fraud, synthetic identity fraud in fintech platforms
- Penalty: imprisonment up to 3 years, or fine up to PKR 5 million, or both

### Unauthorized Interception (Section 19)
- Intercepting electronic communications without authorization
- Covers man-in-the-middle attacks on financial transactions
- Penalty: imprisonment up to 2 years, or fine up to PKR 500,000, or both

## Data Protection Provisions

### Absence of a General Privacy Offence
- PECA 2016 does not create a general right-to-privacy or data-protection offence
- The nearest provisions are unauthorized use of identity information (Section 16),
  offences against the dignity of a natural person (Section 20) and spoofing (Section 26)
- Data protection duties for financial institutions arise from SBP regulations, not from PECA

### Data Breach Reporting
- PECA does not mandate specific data breach notification timelines
- However, SBP regulations require financial institutions to report security incidents
- Since the Prevention of Electronic Crimes (Amendment) Act, 2025 (Act No. II of 2025), offences under PECA are investigated by the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) (Section 29, as substituted)

## Regulatory Bodies Under PECA

### National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA)
- The 2025 amendment substituted Section 29: the Federal Government shall establish an investigation agency called the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) for inquiry into, investigation and prosecution of offences under PECA, headed by a Director General. This replaced the Federal Investigation Agency's role under the Act
- Only an authorized officer of the investigation agency has the power to investigate an offence under PECA; the Federal Government may constitute joint investigation teams of NCCIA and other law enforcement agencies (Section 30, as substituted)
- Receives complaints from individuals and organizations
- Can request data preservation orders from service providers

### Social Media Protection and Regulatory Authority (2025)
- Since the 2025 amendment, "Authority" in PECA means the Social Media Protection and Regulatory Authority (Section 2(iv), as substituted), not the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA)
- The Authority may issue directions to a social media platform for removal or blocking of unlawful or offensive online content (Section 2R)
- PTA remains named in Section 17 (unauthorized issuance of SIM cards)

## Obligations for Fintech Companies

### Expedited Preservation and Acquisition of Data (Section 31)
- Where specific data is reasonably required for a criminal investigation and there is a risk it may be modified, lost, destroyed or rendered inaccessible, an authorised officer may, by written notice to the person in control of the information system, require that person to provide the data, or to preserve it and maintain its integrity, for a period not exceeding ninety days as specified in the notice
- The authorised officer must bring the acquisition of the data to the notice of the Court immediately, and not later than twenty-four hours
- The Court may extend the preservation period on an application from the authorised officer

### Retention of Traffic Data (Section 32)
- A service provider must, within its existing or required technical capability, retain its specified traffic data for a minimum period of one year, or such period as the Authority may notify from time to time
- Subject to production of a warrant issued by the Court, the data must be provided to the investigation agency or the authorised officer whenever required
- The traffic data must be retained in accordance with the data retention and originality requirements of sections 5 and 6 of the Electronic Transactions Ordinance, 2002
- An owner of an information system who is not a licensee of the Authority and violates this retention requirement commits an offence punishable, for a first offence, with a fine which may extend to ten million rupees, and on any subsequent conviction with imprisonment which may extend to six months, or with fine, or both
- A violation by a licensee of the Authority is treated as a violation of its licence terms and conditions under the Pakistan Telecommunication (Re-organization) Act, 1996

### Transaction and Access Records
- Financial institutions should maintain transaction logs and system access records

### Cooperation with Authorities
- Duty to cooperate with authorized officers during investigations
- Powers of investigation are set out in Section 30, with search and seizure under a warrant
  issued under Section 33 and disclosure of content data under Section 34

### Security Measures
- While PECA does not prescribe specific technical controls, the obligation to prevent unauthorized access implies implementation of reasonable security measures
- SBP's Enterprise Technology Governance & Risk Management Framework (BPRD Circular No. 05 of 2017) supplements PECA requirements for financial entities

## Penalties Summary

| Offence | Section | Max Imprisonment | Max Fine |
|---------|---------|-----------------|----------|
| Unauthorized Access | 3 | 3 months | PKR 50,000 |
| Unauthorized Access to Critical Infrastructure | 6 | 3 years | PKR 1 million |
| Data Copying | 4 | 6 months | PKR 100,000 |
| System Interference | 5 | 2 years | PKR 500,000 |
| Electronic Fraud | 14 | 2 years | PKR 10 million |
| Electronic Forgery | 13 | 3 years | PKR 250,000 |
| Identity Theft | 16 | 3 years | PKR 5 million |
| Spoofing | 26 | 3 years | PKR 500,000 |

## Relevance to Fintech Compliance
- Fintech platforms must implement security controls to prevent offences under PECA
- Customer data handling must respect privacy provisions
- Incident response plans should include NCCIA reporting procedures
- Employee awareness training on electronic crimes and data protection
- Third-party agreements should include PECA compliance obligations

# Basel Framework — Basel III and Basel IV

## Overview
The Basel Framework is a set of international banking regulations developed by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) at the Bank for International Settlements (BIS). Basel III was introduced after the 2008 financial crisis to strengthen bank capital requirements, liquidity management, and leverage ratios. Basel IV (finalized December 2017, implementation ongoing through 2028) further reforms credit risk, operational risk, and output floors. These standards are relevant to fintechs that partner with banks or operate as licensed financial institutions.

## Scope
Applies to internationally active banks and, through national implementation, to all deposit-taking institutions. In Pakistan, SBP implements Basel standards for all commercial banks and DFIs. Fintechs partnering with banks must understand these requirements as they affect capital allocation for bank-fintech partnerships.

## Basel III — Key Pillars

### Pillar 1: Minimum Capital Requirements

#### Capital Ratios
- **Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1)**: minimum 4.5% of risk-weighted assets (RWAs)
- **Tier 1 Capital**: minimum 6% of RWAs
- **Total Capital**: minimum 8% of RWAs
- **Capital Conservation Buffer**: additional 2.5% CET1
- **Countercyclical Buffer**: 0-2.5% CET1 (set by national regulators)
- **Systemically Important Bank Surcharge**: 1-3.5% additional CET1

#### Credit Risk
- Standardized Approach: risk weights assigned by asset class and external rating
- Internal Ratings-Based (IRB) Approach: banks use internal models for PD, LGD, EAD
- Fintech lending partnerships: loans originated through fintechs carry risk weights on the bank's balance sheet

#### Operational Risk
- Basic Indicator Approach: 15% of average gross income
- Standardized Approach: business-line-specific percentages
- Advanced Measurement Approaches (replaced by SMA in Basel IV)

#### Market Risk
- Standardized Approach with revised sensitivities-based method
- Internal Models Approach with stricter approval criteria

### Pillar 2: Supervisory Review Process (SREP)
- Banks must maintain capital above minimum requirements based on their risk profile
- Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP)
- Supervisors can impose additional capital buffers
- Stress testing requirements: adverse and severely adverse scenarios

### Pillar 3: Market Discipline
- Public disclosure of capital adequacy, risk exposures, and risk management practices
- Standardized disclosure templates
- Semi-annual and quarterly reporting requirements

## Basel III — Liquidity Standards

### Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)
- Stock of high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) >= 100% of total net cash outflows over 30 days
- Ensures banks can withstand a 30-day liquidity stress scenario
- HQLA categories: Level 1 (cash, central bank reserves), Level 2A (government bonds), Level 2B (corporate bonds, equities)

### Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)
- Available Stable Funding (ASF) >= 100% of Required Stable Funding (RSF)
- Ensures stable long-term funding for less liquid assets
- Promotes use of stable funding sources (deposits, long-term debt) vs. short-term wholesale funding

### Leverage Ratio
- Tier 1 Capital / Total Exposure >= 3%
- Non-risk-based backstop to capital ratios
- Captures on- and off-balance-sheet exposures

## Basel IV — Key Reforms (Implementation 2023-2028)

### Revised Standardized Approach for Credit Risk
- More granular risk weight categories
- Reduced reliance on external credit ratings
- New risk weight tables for real estate, retail, corporate exposures

### Output Floor
- Risk-weighted assets under internal models cannot be lower than 72.5% of the standardized approach RWAs
- Phased implementation: 50% (2023) to 72.5% (2028)

### Revised Operational Risk — Standardized Measurement Approach (SMA)
- Replaces all previous operational risk approaches with a single standardized method
- Based on Business Indicator Component (BIC) and Internal Loss Multiplier (ILM)
- Loss data collection from internal operational loss events

### Credit Valuation Adjustment (CVA) Risk
- Standardized and basic approaches for CVA risk capital
- Captures risk of changes in fair value of derivatives due to counterparty credit quality changes

## Relevance to Fintech

### Bank-Fintech Partnerships
- Banks must hold capital against fintech-originated loans on their balance sheet
- Operational risk capital may increase for banks with significant fintech partnerships
- Third-party risk management requirements apply to fintech service providers

### Digital Banking Licenses
- Digital banks must comply with full Basel requirements
- SBP's digital bank licensing framework incorporates Basel capital and liquidity standards
- Fintech companies seeking banking licenses face Basel compliance obligations

### Cloud and Technology Risk
- Basel Committee guidance on outsourcing and cloud services
- Technology risk included in operational risk framework
- Cyber resilience expectations for financial institutions

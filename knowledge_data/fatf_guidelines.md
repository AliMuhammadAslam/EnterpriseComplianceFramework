# Financial Action Task Force (FATF) — Recommendations and Fintech Guidance

## Overview
The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) is the global inter-governmental body that sets standards and promotes implementation of legal, regulatory, and operational measures for combating money laundering, terrorist financing, and proliferation financing. The FATF Recommendations are recognized as the global AML/CFT standard. FATF conducts mutual evaluations of member jurisdictions and maintains grey/black lists for non-compliant countries.

## Scope
Applies to all countries and their financial institutions, designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs), and virtual asset service providers (VASPs). FATF standards are implemented through national legislation and enforced by national authorities.

## The 40 Recommendations (Summary)

### AML/CFT Policies and Coordination (Recommendations 1-2)
- **R1**: Risk-based approach — countries must assess ML/TF risks and apply resources accordingly
- **R2**: National cooperation and coordination among policy-makers, FIU, law enforcement, and regulators

### Money Laundering and Confiscation (Recommendations 3-4)
- **R3**: Criminalization of money laundering consistent with Vienna and Palermo Conventions
- **R4**: Confiscation and provisional measures — freezing, seizing, and confiscating proceeds of crime

### Terrorist Financing and Proliferation (Recommendations 5-8)
- **R5**: Criminalization of terrorist financing
- **R6**: Targeted financial sanctions related to terrorism and terrorist financing (UNSCR 1267/1373)
- **R7**: Targeted financial sanctions related to proliferation (UNSCR on Iran and DPRK)
- **R8**: Non-profit organizations — measures to prevent misuse for TF

### Preventive Measures (Recommendations 9-23)
- **R10**: Customer Due Diligence — identification, verification, beneficial ownership, ongoing monitoring
- **R11**: Record keeping — 5-year minimum retention
- **R12**: Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) — enhanced measures for foreign and domestic PEPs
- **R13**: Correspondent banking — enhanced due diligence for cross-border relationships
- **R14**: Money or value transfer services — licensing/registration, compliance monitoring
- **R15**: New technologies and virtual assets — risk-based regulation of VASPs, Travel Rule
- **R16**: Wire transfers — originator and beneficiary information requirements
- **R17-R19**: Reliance on third parties, internal controls, countries with higher risk
- **R20**: Suspicious transaction reporting to FIU
- **R21**: Tipping-off and confidentiality
- **R22-R23**: DNFBPs — CDD and reporting obligations

### Transparency and Beneficial Ownership (Recommendations 24-25)
- **R24**: Transparency of legal persons — beneficial ownership registries
- **R25**: Transparency of legal arrangements (trusts)

### Powers of Authorities (Recommendations 26-35)
- **R26-R28**: FIU role, law enforcement powers, investigative authority
- **R29**: Financial intelligence unit — operational independence, information exchange
- **R30-R32**: Responsibilities of authorities, sanctions, statistics
- **R33-R34**: International cooperation — mutual legal assistance, extradition
- **R35**: Mutual legal assistance for freezing and confiscation

### International Cooperation (Recommendations 36-40)
- **R36-R40**: Information exchange, mutual legal assistance, extradition, other forms of cooperation

## FATF Guidance on Virtual Assets and VASPs (Updated 2021)

### Key Requirements
- Countries must assess and mitigate risks associated with virtual asset activities
- VASPs must be licensed or registered
- VASPs subject to AML/CFT obligations: CDD, record keeping, STR filing
- Travel Rule (R16) applies to VASPs: originator and beneficiary information for VA transfers exceeding USD/EUR 1,000
- Supervision of VASPs by competent authorities
- Sanctions for non-compliance including criminal penalties

### Definition of Virtual Assets
- Digital representation of value that can be digitally traded, transferred, or used for payment
- Does not include digital representations of fiat currencies, securities, or other financial assets already covered

## FATF Guidance on Digital Identity (2020)

### Key Principles
- Digital identity systems can be reliable for CDD if they provide adequate assurance of identity
- Risk-based approach to accepting digital identity
- Government-issued digital identity systems generally provide higher assurance
- Biometric verification, video verification, and e-KYC can satisfy CDD requirements
- Standards for assessing digital identity reliability: governance, security, enrollment integrity

## FATF and Pakistan

### Mutual Evaluation
- Pakistan underwent FATF mutual evaluation (Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering — APG)
- Pakistan was on the FATF grey list (2018-2022) for strategic AML/CFT deficiencies
- Action Plan items included: demonstrating effective TF investigations, sanctions enforcement, and international cooperation
- Pakistan completed its FATF Action Plan and was removed from the grey list in October 2022

### Implications for Pakistani Fintechs
- Heightened regulatory scrutiny on AML/CFT compliance
- Strict enforcement of sanctions screening
- Enhanced reporting to FMU
- Regular compliance audits by SBP and SECP
- Need for robust transaction monitoring systems

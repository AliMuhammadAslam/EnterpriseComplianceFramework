# Pakistan Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT)

## Overview
Pakistan's AML/CFT framework is governed by the Anti-Money Laundering Act 2010 (AMLA), the Anti-Terrorism Act 1997, and regulations issued by the State Bank of Pakistan and SECP. Pakistan's Financial Monitoring Unit (FMU) serves as the national Financial Intelligence Unit. This framework is critical for fintech compliance, particularly given Pakistan's FATF evaluation history and ongoing mutual evaluation commitments.

## Key Legislation

### Anti-Money Laundering Act 2010 (AMLA)
- Criminalizes money laundering with penalties of imprisonment (1-10 years) and fines
- Provides for the Financial Monitoring Unit (FMU), established by the Federal Government by notification and housed in SBP or at any other place in Pakistan (section 6)
- Defines reporting obligations for financial institutions and DNFBPs
- Provides for freezing and confiscation of proceeds of crime

### Anti-Terrorism Act 1997 (as amended)
- Criminalizes terror financing: the offences are in sections 11H to 11K, and section 11N (as amended in 2020) sets the punishment: imprisonment for a term not less than five years and not exceeding ten years and a fine not exceeding PKR 25 million; a legal person is liable to a fine not exceeding PKR 50 million
- Proscription of organizations involved in terrorism
- Seizure and forfeiture of terrorist property
- Counter-financing obligations for financial institutions

## Customer Due Diligence (CDD) Requirements

### Standard CDD
- Verify customer identity using reliable, independent documents (CNIC, passport)
- Verify beneficial ownership for legal entities
- Understand the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship
- Ongoing monitoring of transactions consistent with customer risk profile

### Simplified Due Diligence (SDD)
- Permitted only where low risk is identified (SBP AML/CFT/CPF Regulation 2(18))
- SDD measures may include verifying identity after the business relationship is established, reducing the degree of ongoing monitoring, and inferring the purpose and nature of the relationship from the type of transactions
- Cannot be applied where ML/TF suspicion exists

### Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)
- Required for high-risk customers, PEPs, and correspondent banking relationships
- Additional identity verification measures
- Senior management approval for establishing or continuing the relationship
- Source of funds and source of wealth verification
- Increased frequency and depth of transaction monitoring

### Digital / Electronic KYC
- In digital channels, SBP-regulated entities shall use, wherever required, mitigation measures against identity theft such as live picture or video, electronic / digital signature, two-factor authentication, and biometric verification of customers (Regulation 12(5))
- All SBP-regulated entities shall implement automated Transaction Monitoring Systems (Regulation 12(6))

## Reporting Obligations

### Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs)
- Financial institutions must file STRs with the FMU when there is reasonable ground to suspect ML/TF
- No monetary threshold for STRs — based on suspicion
- STRs must be filed with the FMU promptly (AMLA section 7(1), as amended in 2020; SBP AML/CFT/CPF Regulation 7 requires STRs to be filed as required under section 7 of the Act)
- Tipping off the customer is prohibited

### Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs)
- Cash transactions exceeding PKR 2 million (or equivalent in foreign currency) must be reported to FMU
- Only cash transactions are reported as CTRs (FMU Guidelines for the Reporting Entities on filing of Currency Transaction Reports, 2021)
- Must be filed with the FMU immediately, but not later than seven working days, after the respective currency transaction (AMLA section 7(3))

### International Fund Transfer Reports
- Wire / fund transfers must identify and verify the originator and carry originator and beneficiary information (SBP AML/CFT/CPF Regulation 11; FATF Recommendation 16)

## Record Keeping
- CDD records (identification data, account files and business correspondence): at least five years following the termination of the business relationship under AMLA section 7C; SBP-regulated entities must keep them for ten years after the business relationship is ended (SBP AML/CFT/CPF Regulations, Regulation 8(2))
- Transaction records: at least five years following the completion of the transaction under AMLA section 7C; SBP-regulated entities must keep them for a minimum of ten years from completion of the transaction (Regulation 8(3))
- Records of STRs and CTRs: at least ten years after reporting of the transaction (AMLA section 7(4)); SBP-regulated entities must keep them for a minimum of ten years from completion of the transaction (Regulation 8(3))
- Records must be sufficient to reconstruct individual transactions
- Records must be available to competent authorities upon request

## Risk-Based Approach
- Financial institutions must conduct institutional ML/TF risk assessment
- Customer risk categorization: Low, Medium, High
- Risk factors: customer type, geography, product/service, delivery channel
- The internal risk assessment (IRAR) is kept up to date by updating it periodically when circumstances change or new threats emerge, and when the National Risk Assessment is updated (Regulation 1(7))
- Board-approved AML/CFT policy reflecting risk assessment outcomes

## Sanctions Screening
- Screening against United Nations Security Council sanctions lists
- Screening against Pakistan's national proscribed persons/entities lists
- Real-time screening of customers and occasional customers against designated and proscribed persons (Regulation 4(3))

## Compliance Program Requirements
- Board-level AML/CFT oversight
- Designated Compliance Officer
- Written AML/CFT policies, procedures, and controls
- An independent audit function, adequately resourced, that regularly assesses the effectiveness of AML/CFT/CPF policies, procedures and controls (Internal Controls 5)
- An Annual Training Program developed after a formal training need assessment (Internal Controls 11)
- Screening of employees during hiring
